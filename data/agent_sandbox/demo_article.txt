The Printing Press

Johannes Gutenberg invented the movable-type printing press in Mainz, Germany, around 1440. Before this, books were copied by hand, a slow and expensive process that limited how many people could own or read them.

Gutenberg's press combined oil-based ink, a wooden press adapted from wine presses, and reusable metal type pieces cast from a lead alloy. This let printers set a full page of text from individual letters, print many copies quickly, and then reuse the same pieces of type for the next page.

The technology spread rapidly across Europe. Within about 50 years, printing presses were operating in more than 200 cities. Historians credit the printing press with helping spread literacy, standardizing languages, and enabling the rapid distribution of ideas during the Renaissance and Reformation.
